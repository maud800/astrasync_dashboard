Summary

AstraSync must decide how to price an 18-month AI/ML implementation after the client rejected its $160,000-per-month proposal and countered with two offers: equal payments of $152,000 (Option A), or $125,000 plus an $800,000 success bonus (Option B); refusing both sends the project to an RFP (Option C). We valued each option as the present value of AstraSync’s monthly margin over its $120,000 monthly cost at a 1.5% monthly discount rate, using a decision tree and 1,000,000 Monte Carlo simulations of the client’s triangular savings to value the RFP gain-share. The expected values are $501,521.9 for A, $506,713.3 for B and $509,431.4 for C, so the RFP has the highest expected value, but by only $2,718 over B. That small edge comes with a 60% chance of earning nothing, and it disappears if AstraSync’s chance of winning the RFP falls below 39.8%. We recommend AstraSync accept Option B, which gives up less than 1% of expected value in exchange for a guaranteed contract, a much smaller spread of outcomes and a bonus that rewards the implementation success the team already expects.

Analysis

Each option is valued from AstraSync’s side: monthly price minus the $120,000 monthly cost (1,200 hours at $100), discounted with the annuity factor AF = 15.6726. Single payments at month 18 are discounted with DF = 0.7649.

Option A: equal payments

$501,521.9

The client pays $152,000 every month for 18 months. There is no uncertainty, so the expected value is the present value.

($152,000 − $120,000) × 15.6726 = $501,521.9
  • Guaranteed margin of $32,000 per month.
  • No upside if the project outperforms.

Option B: payments plus bonus

$506,713.3

The client pays $125,000 per month and an $800,000 bonus at month 18 if the implementation beats the benchmark (probability 0.7).

Margin: $5,000 × AF = $78,362.8
Bonus: $800,000 × DF = $611,929.3
EV = $78,362.8 + 0.7 × $611,929.3 = $506,713.3
  • Outcomes: $690,292.1 (70%) or $78,362.8 (30%).
  • Standard deviation about $280.4K.

Option C: RFP with gain-share

$509,431.4

AstraSync bids $150,000 per month against competitors (win probability 0.4) and, if it wins, also receives a share of the client’s savings at month 18.

If won: $30,000 × AF + E[share] × DF
= $470,176.8 + $1,050,319.5 × 0.7649 = $1,273,578.4
EV = 0.4 × $1,273,578.4 = $509,431.4
  • 60% chance of $0; best case about $3.59M.
  • Standard deviation about $766.0K.

Simulated gain-share under Option C

Client savings follow a triangular distribution (low $3.2M, most likely $5.6M, high $12.8M). In 2.8% of runs savings fall below $4M and the share is $0. The long right tail pulls the mean ($1,050,319.5) above the median ($766,499.5).

Sensitivity analysis

The two inputs that matter most are the probabilities that drive the uncertain options: the chance of winning the RFP (C) and the chance of earning the bonus (B). Both are estimates, and the ranking of the options turns on each of them.

Option C’s value rises by $12,736 for every percentage point of win probability. It beats B only above 39.8% and beats A above 39.4%. The base case of 40% sits barely above the break-even.

Option B’s value rises by $6,119 for every percentage point of bonus probability. It beats A above 69.2% and beats C above 70.4%. The base case of 70% is within one point of both thresholds.

The base case sits right on the corner where all three regions meet, so a one- or two-point change in either probability changes which option has the highest expected value. Because the expected values are effectively tied, the decision should rest on risk: B and A guarantee a contract, C does not.

Conclusion

By expected value alone, the RFP (Option C, $509,431.4) narrowly beats Option B ($506,713.3) and Option A ($501,521.9), and all three fall short of the original proposal ($626,902.4). The margin between them is less than 2% of their value, and the sensitivity analysis shows the ranking flips with very small changes in the win or bonus probabilities, which are themselves estimates.

Risk therefore decides. Option C has a 60% chance of producing nothing, a standard deviation of about $766.0K, and exposes AstraSync to competitors on a client it already serves. Option B guarantees the contract, limits the downside to $78,362.8, and turns the team’s confidence in a successful implementation into a 70% chance of $690,292.1. AstraSync should accept Option B. It should prefer the RFP only if it has strong evidence that its chance of winning is well above 40%, and should prefer Option A if it doubts the bonus probability is at least 69%.
